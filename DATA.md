# Data provenance

## What it is

| File | Rows | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `data/incidents.csv` | 360 | Main dataset, 6 categories (v1 taxonomy) |
| `data/incidents_v2.csv` | 80 | Changed-requirement set: 40 `temperature_excursion` + 40 old-category reports |
| `data/review/review_sheet.csv` | 60 | Blind sheet for the independent label review |

Columns: `id`, `split` (template-held-out train/val/test), `split_random` (random train/val/test),
`text`, `category`, `urgency` (`urgent`/`normal`), `difficulty` (`easy`/`hard`), `template_id`.

## Where it comes from

**Synthetic.** Every report is generated by `data/generate.py` (fixed seed 2049, so it is
byte-for-byte reproducible). No client, employee or customer data is used. Customer names (Northwind,
Contoso, Fabrikam, ...) are the well-known fictional sample-company names; sites are generic
UK/EU logistics locations; shipment IDs are random.

Each report = a hand-written **template** (82 in total) + random slot values (site, cargo, customer,
vehicle, times, temperatures) + light noise (reporter prefix such as "Driver report:", occasional
typos, occasional all-lowercase). The label belongs to the template and was assigned when the
template was written, using the guide below.

Roughly a fifth of templates are **hard on purpose**:
- angry but not urgent ("THIS IS UNACCEPTABLE!!! Third time it's late...") → `normal`
- calm but urgent ("Small thing, but the insulin hasn't left yet and it's due in 50 minutes") → `urgent`
- multi-issue reports where the precedence rule decides (damaged hazmat box that is smoking → `safety_incident`)
- past events already handled (theft discovered and reported to police) → `normal`

## Splits

- `split` (**primary**): split **by template**, stratified by (category, urgency): 208 train / 60 val /
  92 test. No test report shares a template with a training report, so it measures generalisation to
  new wording. A random row split lets TF-IDF memorise templates. We keep it as `split_random`
  (217/53/90) to show the gap.
- Model selection (TF-IDF hyper-parameters), probability calibration and the review threshold use
  **val only**. Test is touched once, by `scripts/evaluate.py`.

## Labelling guide (used for templates and for the independent review)

**Category** (exactly one):

| category | definition |
|---|---|
| delivery_delay | late, missed slot, stuck in a queue or in traffic, rescheduling; no damage or loss |
| damaged_goods | cargo or packaging physically damaged; no injury, no hazardous release |
| lost_or_stolen | missing, mis-delivered, not scanned, theft, break-in, seal tampering |
| safety_incident | any injury, near miss, fire, hazardous leak/spill, unsafe site; **takes precedence** |
| vehicle_equipment_failure | truck, trailer, forklift, reefer, scanner, conveyor breakdown; no injury |
| documentation_customs | missing or incorrect paperwork, customs hold, invoice/label mismatch, permits |
| temperature_excursion *(v2 only)* | temperature-controlled cargo outside its allowed range |

**Urgency**: `urgent` = needs action within 1 hour. That means a person is hurt or in danger, hazardous
material has been released, a road/dock/site is blocked, a theft is in progress, or perishable/medical
cargo will be lost without immediate action. Everything else is `normal`, **regardless of tone,
capital letters or the word "urgent"**.

## Known limitations

- Template-generated text is less varied than real reports (no long email threads, no
  attachments, few languages). Real performance will be lower. See BUSINESS_SUMMARY.md.
- The keyword baseline (`triage/rules.py`) was written by the same person who wrote the templates,
  so its scores are optimistic.
- Labels follow one person's labelling guide; the independent review (below) measures how
  reproducible that guide is.

## Independent label review

`python scripts/label_review.py sheet` drew 60 reports (20 hard + 40 across categories) into
`data/review/review_sheet.csv` with labels hidden. A reviewer labels them using only this guide, then
`python scripts/label_review.py score` writes agreement, Cohen's kappa and every disagreement to
`results/LABEL_REVIEW.md`.
